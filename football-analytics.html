Football Analytics App

A mobile app prototype that helps casual football fans understand stats like expected goals (xG). This was my final year project at university.

Role UX Designer Type Final Year Project Year 2026 Tools Figma, Miro, Google Forms
Problem

Most football apps are made for people who already understand the data. Casual fans see stats like xG with no explanation and don't know what they mean.

What I did

Surveyed 50 fans, looked at SofaScore, FotMob and Flashscore, then designed and tested a mobile prototype.

Result

Scored 5.0/5 for ease of use, and all five testers completed every task.

Research

What I found from fans.

I ran an online survey with 50 football fans and looked at three of the most popular stats apps. These were the main things I learned.

Fans struggled to understand advanced metricsSo every complex stat needed a simple explanation next to it.
Fans cared most about player statsSo player information needed to be easy to find.
Most fans used their phoneSo I designed for mobile first.
Charts helped people understand dataSo I used charts and visuals instead of big tables of numbers.
Competitor analysis

What other apps were doing.

SofaScoreHas lots of detail, like shots, xG and heatmaps, but doesn't explain how to read any of it. The screens are packed with information, which is a lot to take in on a phone.
FotMobClean and quick to read during live games, but the more advanced stats are hidden away and some aren't explained.

I wanted to keep FotMob's clean, simple layout, but add visuals and explanations that help fans understand the numbers, without showing everything at once.

Who I designed for

David, a regular fan.

David watches football every week and knows the game well. He likes stats when they help explain why a team played well, and enjoys talking about them with his friends.

Pain points

  • Raw data and stats without explanation
  • Confusing technical terms

What he needs

  • Visual explanations of performance
  • Simple definitions for advanced metrics
  • Easy player and match comparisons
Key decisions

Key design decisions.

I turned the research into a list of user requirements, then focused on the ones that would help a fan like David the most.

Decision 01 / 03

Explain each stat with an info button

What I found
The biggest thing I found was that fans didn't understand complex match data, and other apps didn't explain it.
What I did
I added an info button next to each stat. Tapping it opens a pop-up that explains what the stat is, what it shows and how to read it. Fans who already understand it can just ignore the button.
The info pop-ups

Every pop-up uses the same layout: what the stat is, what it shows and how to read it, with a “Got it” button to close.

Decision 02 / 03

Use charts to show how a match played out

What I found
Fans found charts helpful and wanted to understand why a team played well, not just see the final numbers.
What I did
I added a match momentum chart that shows which team was on top at each point in the game.
Decision 03 / 03

Split each match into tabs

What I found
SofaScore's screens felt overwhelming, while FotMob's simpler layout was much easier to read quickly.
What I did
Each match has Overview, Lineups, Statistics and Standings tabs, so fans only see one section at a time.
Testing

Testing the prototype.

Five people tested the prototype. They found a match, looked up a player and explained what a stat meant, then rated the app in a questionnaire.

People liked the simple navigation and the explanations most. The explanations still scored lowest of the four areas, so that's what I'd improve next.

5.0/5

Ease of use. All five people completed every task.

4.75/5

How clear the navigation was.

4.5/5

4.25/5

How helpful the stat explanations were.

Reflection

What I learned and what's next.

Basing each decision on research made the design easier to explain and to test.

Next, I'd test with more people, improve the explanations, and look at adding live data and more personalisation.
