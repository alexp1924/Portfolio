Overview

Designing for the world's biggest sporting moments.

During my placement year at Hawk-Eye Innovations, a global sports technology company, I designed operator-facing tools used in professional sport, working closely with product managers, developers and the operators themselves.

I worked on three tools. The cricket no-ball panel is covered in depth below, because it shows my process from research through to a shipped product.

Project 01 / 03 · Cricket

Cricket “No Ball” Review Panel

During live matches, operators process no-ball reviews before a replay goes out. They need to select the delivery, identify the no-ball type, choose the batter and confirm the original umpire decision, all under live broadcast conditions.

I designed a dedicated panel for this inside Hawk-Eye's existing cricket review system.

Problem

Operators make four separate decisions under live broadcast pressure before a no-ball replay can be sent.

What I did

Spoke with operators to understand their frustrations, then refined the panel with them through rounds of feedback.

Result

Deployed in the IPL as part of the cricket review workflow.

Research

Designing it with the operators, not just for them.

I held meetings with operators over Google Meet and stayed in regular contact with them over messages. I wanted to understand what they needed from a no-ball review, and what frustrated them about how it worked at the time.

From there I went back and forth with designs, taking each version to the operators for their opinion and adjusting the panel based on their feedback.

Decision 01 / 04

A numbered workflow that mirrors the review

What I found
The review follows a strict sequence of steps, and missing a critical input before the replay is sent is a real risk.
What I did
I built the panel around a numbered workflow, so operators always know what information is still required.
Decision 02 / 04

Warnings before a mistake goes out

What I found
Not every outcome can be calculated immediately during a live review.
What I did
Validation and warning states highlight missing or incomplete information, so issues are resolved before a replay is sent.
Decision 03 / 04

One decision at a time

What I found
Operators make four separate decisions, and presenting every option at once competes for their attention.
What I did
Related actions are grouped into stages: trajectory selection, no-ball type, batter selection and original decision.
Decision 04 / 04

Familiar patterns, less retraining

What I found
Operators already use an established review product every match.
What I did
The panel follows its existing interaction patterns and visual conventions, which reduced training and helped operators adopt the new workflow quickly.
Outcome

Shipped and deployed in the IPL.

The final design was incorporated into the cricket review workflow and has since been deployed in the IPL (Indian Premier League).

Project 02 / 03 · Basketball

Basketball Status API Feed Tool

A tool for operators to manage the status of live basketball games and report data issues in real time, without disrupting ongoing live operations.

Prioritising game visibilityA card-based game list shows every game's status at a glance, so operators watching several games can spot the one that needs attention.
Keeping users in contextStatus updates and error reports open in a modal over the game list, so operators never lose sight of the wider schedule.
Separating operational tasksUpdating status and reporting issues sit in separate tabs, reducing cognitive load in time-sensitive moments.
Providing clear feedbackConfirmation messages show an update has gone through, because changes affect downstream systems.
Project 03 / 03 · Tennis

Tennis Logging Tool

A tablet-based logging tool for coaches and players at training facilities, covering session setup, player configuration, drill selection and live tracking, simple enough to use courtside during practice.

Separating setup from live trackingConfiguration happens up front, so the tracking screen stays simple during practice.
Guiding session creationPlayers, rackets, drill type and court position are entered step by step, in a logical order.
Designing for tablet useLarge touch targets and clear primary actions for quick courtside use.
Emphasising critical actionsDuring a drill, start, stop and end tracking dominate; secondary controls are de-emphasised.
