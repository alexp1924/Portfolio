Football Analytics App

A mobile-first prototype that helps casual football fans understand advanced metrics like expected goals (xG), through clear visual hierarchy, contextual explanations and intuitive navigation.

Role UX Designer Type Final Year Project Year 2026 Tools Figma, Miro, Google Forms
Problem

Football apps are built for analysts. Casual fans see metrics like xG with no explanation and give up on them.

What I did

Surveyed 50 fans, analysed SofaScore, FotMob and Flashscore, then designed and tested a mobile prototype.

Result

5.0/5 for ease of use. All five testers completed every task.

Research

What fans told me, and what the market was missing.

I ran an online survey with 50 football fans and analysed three of the most-used stats apps. Four findings shaped everything that followed.

Fans struggled to understand advanced metricsEvery complex stat needed a plain-language explanation, available in context.
Player statistics were what fans wanted mostPlayer information had to be the easiest thing to find.
Mobile was the most common deviceThe design had to be mobile-first, with touch-friendly, readable visuals.
Visualisations helped people understand dataCharts and timelines over dense tables, simple by default with detail on request.
Competitor analysis

One app was too dense. The other hid the depth.

SofaScoreVery detailed coverage of shots, xG and heatmaps, but it relies on numbers without guidance on how to read them. Information-dense screens lead to cognitive overload, especially on mobile.
FotMobClean, glanceable and fast for live matches, but advanced stats are hidden behind a button and some data lacks explanation.

The opportunity: combine FotMob's immediacy and clear hierarchy with richer visualisations that explain what the numbers mean, layering advanced data so it is never all shown at once.

Who I designed for

David, a regular fan who wants to know why.

David watches football every week and knows the game well. He enjoys stats when they explain performance, and wants talking points for discussions with his friends.

Pain points

  • Raw data and stats without explanation
  • Overly technical terminology that is confusing

What he needs

  • Visual explanations of performance
  • Simple definitions for advanced metrics
  • Easy player and match comparisons
Key decisions

Three decisions, each traced back to the research.

From the findings I wrote a set of user requirements, then prioritised the ones that would make the biggest difference to a fan like David.

Decision 01 / 03

Simple stats first, advanced metrics explained on demand

What I found
Fans struggled with advanced metrics, and both competitors presented numbers without guidance.
What I did
Familiar stats such as possession and shots lead each match. xG sits alongside them with an info icon that explains it in plain language, so confident fans aren't slowed down.
Decision 02 / 03

Charts that tell the story of a match

What I found
Fans found visualisations helpful, and wanted to understand why a team or player performed well, not just the final numbers.
What I did
A match momentum chart shows how the game swung over time, with a “What is this?” prompt for anyone unfamiliar with it.
Decision 03 / 03

Break the match into focused tabs

What I found
SofaScore's information-dense screens caused cognitive overload, while FotMob's clear hierarchy worked well under time pressure.
What I did
Each match splits into Overview, Lineups, Statistics and Standings tabs, with grouped content cards and a bottom navigation bar, so fans see one layer of detail at a time.
Testing

Did it actually help fans understand the data?

Five participants completed three tasks: finding a match, finding information about a player, and interpreting a statistic. They then rated the prototype in a questionnaire.

Simple navigation and the contextual explanations received the most positive feedback. Explanations still scored lowest of the four areas, so that is where I would focus the next round of iteration.

5.0/5

Ease of use. All five participants completed all tasks successfully.

4.75/5

Navigation clarity and overall information architecture.

4.5/5

Visual hierarchy across screens and data presentations.

4.25/5

Contextual explanations for complex metrics including xG.

Reflection

What I learned, and what comes next.

Grounding each decision in research made the design easier to explain and easier to test.

Next, I would test with a larger and more varied group than five fans, refine the explanations based on their lower score, and explore live data and greater personalisation.
