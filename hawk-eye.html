Overview

Designing for the world's biggest sporting moments.

During my placement year at Hawk-Eye Innovations, a global sports technology company, I worked as a UX/UI Designer — helping design digital products used in professional sports, turning complex data and tracking systems into clear and easy-to-use interfaces.

I worked closely with product managers and developers to understand user needs, create wireframes and prototypes, and improve designs through testing and feedback. This experience helped me learn how to design practical, user-focused solutions while working within real technical and business constraints.

Project 01 / 03

Basketball Status API Feed Tool

A tool that allows operators to manage the status of live basketball games and report data issues in real time. The challenge was designing an interface that supports quick updates while minimising disruption to ongoing live operations.

Prioritising Game VisibilityOperators often need to monitor multiple live games simultaneously. I chose a card-based game list that surfaces status information at a glance, allowing users to quickly identify which games require attention without opening individual views.
Keeping Users in ContextRather than navigating away from the game list, status updates and error reporting are handled within a modal. This allows operators to complete tasks while maintaining awareness of the wider schedule.
Separating Operational TasksUpdating game status and reporting issues serve different user goals, so they were separated into dedicated tabs. This reduces cognitive load and helps users focus on the task at hand during time-sensitive situations.
Providing Clear FeedbackBecause changes can affect downstream systems, it was important that users understood when actions had been completed successfully. Confirmation messages were included to provide confidence that updates had been processed.
Reflection

Small decisions matter when every game is live.

This project reinforced the importance of designing for efficiency in operational environments. Small decisions such as keeping users in context and reducing navigation can have a significant impact when users are managing multiple live events simultaneously.

Project 02 / 03

Cricket "No Ball" Review Panel

As part of the existing cricket review system, I designed a dedicated panel for processing no-ball reviews during live matches. Operators needed to complete a series of decisions before submitting the replay — selecting the delivery, identifying the no-ball type, choosing the batter, and confirming the original umpire decision. The primary challenge was ensuring the process remained simple and reliable under live broadcast conditions.

Guiding Users Through a Fixed WorkflowThe review process follows a strict sequence of steps, so the interface was designed around a numbered workflow. This helps operators understand what information is required and reduces the risk of missing critical inputs before submitting a review.
Progressive ValidationNot every outcome can be calculated immediately. Validation and warning states were introduced to highlight missing or incomplete information, allowing issues to be resolved before a replay is sent.
Reducing Decision ComplexityRather than presenting all available options at once, related actions were grouped into individual stages — trajectory selection, no-ball type, batter selection, and original decision. This keeps attention focused on a single task at a time.
Maintaining Consistency with Existing ToolsAs the panel was being integrated into an established review product, the design followed existing interaction patterns and visual conventions. This reduced training requirements and helped operators adopt the new workflow quickly.
Outcome

Deployed in the IPL.

The final design was incorporated into the cricket review workflow and has since been deployed in the IPL (Indian Premier League).

Project 03 / 03

Tennis Logging Tool

A tablet-based logging tool for coaches and players at training facilities. The product needed to support session setup, player configuration, drill selection, and live tracking — while remaining simple enough to use courtside during an active practice session.

Separating Setup from Live TrackingThe experience was split into two phases: session configuration and active drill tracking. This allowed users to focus on entering required information upfront before moving into a simplified tracking interface during practice.
Guiding Users Through Session CreationCreating a session required multiple pieces of information — players, rackets, drill type, and court positioning. A step-by-step workflow was introduced to reduce complexity and help users complete setup in a logical order.
Designing for Tablet UseAs the tool was intended for courtside use, interactions were designed with large touch targets and clear primary actions. This helps users operate the system quickly without needing precise input.
Emphasising Critical Actions During TrackingOnce a drill begins, the interface shifts focus to the most important actions: starting, stopping, and ending tracking. Secondary controls remain available but are visually de-emphasised to reduce distraction during live sessions.
Outcome

A focused tool for a fast-paced coaching environment.

The final design provided a structured workflow for configuring training sessions while keeping the active tracking experience focused and easy to operate in a fast-paced coaching environment.
